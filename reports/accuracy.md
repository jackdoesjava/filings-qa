# Answer accuracy

Generated by scripts/06_score.py from 16 answer files in results/answers/.

62 of the 150 questions have a gold answer that is a single number (50 metrics-generated, 3 domain-relevant, 9 novel-generated). Those are graded automatically: correct if a number on the reply's `Answer:` line is within 1% of the gold, or within half a unit of the gold's last decimal place (some golds are given to one significant figure), with 25% and 0.25 counted as equal. A money figure off by a factor of a thousand, million or billion is counted wrong and labelled a unit error. For capex and dividends, a minus sign copied from the cash flow statement does not count against the answer. Two different figures of the same kind joined by 'or' are a hedge and count as wrong. The strict column asks for more: the figure and the gold have to agree at the coarser of their two precisions, so 31.0% no longer passes for a gold of 30.8%, though 63.9 days still passes for 63.86. Trailing zeros in a money gold are not counted as precision, because FinanceBench pads money golds to two decimals ($1616.00 for a filing's $1,615.9 million). The rest are graded by qwen2.5:7b with a strict rubric. It sees the question, the gold answer and its justification and the reply, never which config or model wrote it. Intervals are 95% percentile bootstraps over questions (2,000 resamples).

Gold answers that disagree with the filing are corrected before grading (05718: $0.40 is graded as $0.39; the reasons are in GOLD_FIXES in fqa/score.py).

## Judge against the automatic grade

The judge also marked 992 numerically graded answers, where the automatic grade is the reference. It agrees on 93.6 [91.2, 95.8]% of them (Cohen's kappa 0.81, correct versus not). Of the 21 unit errors it accepted 6. Agreement here is the best evidence there is for trusting its verdicts on the other questions, which are harder to judge. The same question comes up once per config and model, so the interval resamples questions, each with all of its answers. Kappa is 0 when either of the two gives every answer the same mark, and blank when both mark every answer correct or both mark every answer wrong.
